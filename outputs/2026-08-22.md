<h2>Understanding the Nutritional Profile of Sprouted Millets</h2>
<p>Millets such as ragi (finger millet) and jowar (sorghum) have long been valued for their resilience in arid climates. Their nutritional merit, however, extends far beyond agricultural hardiness. When these grains undergo controlled sprouting, enzymatic activity breaks down anti-nutritional factors like phytic acid and tannins, which otherwise bind minerals and inhibit absorption. <h3>Why Sprouting Matters for Nutrient Bioavailability</h3>
<p>Research indicates that sprouting ragi can elevate calcium bioavailability by up to 20 percent while reducing phytate content significantly. These changes are not marginal; they redefine the grain from a staple carbohydrate into a functional food ingredient.</p>

<h3>The Role of Complementary Proteins in Plant-Based Diets</h3>
<p>Cereals are typically limited in lysine, while legumes lack methionine.
