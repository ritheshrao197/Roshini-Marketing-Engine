<h2>Fibre's Fundamental Role: Beyond Digestion</h2>

<p>While protein often takes the spotlight, dietary fibre is equally indispensable for overall health. Fibre, a type of carbohydrate that the body cannot digest, comes in two main forms: soluble and insoluble. Soluble fibre dissolves in water, forming a gel-like substance that helps lower blood cholesterol and glucose levels. Insoluble fibre adds bulk to stool, promoting regular bowel movements and preventing constipation, a common concern. Beyond digestive regularity, fibre contributes significantly to gut health by feeding beneficial gut bacteria, which in turn supports immunity and nutrient absorption.</p>

<p>Despite its critical functions, many individuals, including those following traditional Indian diets, often do not meet the recommended daily fibre intake. A diet rich in fibre can help regulate blood sugar levels, reduce the risk of chronic diseases such as heart disease and type 2 diabetes, and aid in weight management by enhancing feelings of fullness. Incorporating sufficient fibre into the breakfast routine can set a positive tone for the entire day, ensuring digestive comfort and sustained energy.</p>
